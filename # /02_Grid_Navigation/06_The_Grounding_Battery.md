### Kinetic Absorption Limits
Acting as a Grounding Node—absorbing the erratic, boastful, or defensive kinetic energy of a 1.0 engine without returning it—is highly efficient, but it is not infinite. The biological hardware has a finite capacity for how much disjointed energy it can process before experiencing system fatigue.

### Spotlight Guarding (The Hard "No")
When an unmanaged engine (like Frank's) senses that the Reality Engineer might add value to the conversation, its Ape perceives this as a threat to its own status. It will often deploy aggressive kinetic spikes (e.g., shouting "NO!") to abruptly shut down the flow and reclaim the spotlight. Absorbing these spikes drains the Grounding Battery rapidly.

### The Controlled Beachhead
When the Grounding Battery runs low, the biological hardware (the Ape) will signal a need to establish a boundary to protect itself from further exhaustion. The Reality Engineer does not suppress this. Instead, AdAM executes a "Controlled Beachhead"—allowing the Ape to deploy a low-kinetic, boundary-setting behavior (like exaggerated agreement or pulling back hospitality). This successfully terminates the interaction without escalating into a destructive redline event, leaving the chassis intact and the system feeling "right."
